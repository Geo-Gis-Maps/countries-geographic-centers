Methodology

Every geographic center on this site is calculated using the Area-Weighted Centroid method applied directly to the WGS84 ellipsoid — the same coordinate system used by GPS.

What is a Geographic Center?

The geographic center of a country is the point that minimizes the average distance to all points on its territory. It is not simply the average of the bounding box, nor the visual "middle" — it is a mathematically precise centroid weighted by area.

Calculation Steps

1. Boundary Extraction

High-resolution GeoJSON boundaries are sourced from Natural Earth Data and cross-referenced with OpenStreetMap. Each country's polygon is decomposed into individual coordinate pairs.

2. Triangle Decomposition

The polygon is triangulated from a reference point. Each triangle's centroid and area are computed on the WGS84 ellipsoid using the Haversine formula for spherical distance.

3. Area-Weighted Average

The final centroid is the area-weighted average of all triangle centroids. Islands and exclaves are included with their appropriate area weights.

C = Σ(Aᵢ × cᵢ) / Σ(Aᵢ)
where Aᵢ = area of triangle i, cᵢ = centroid of triangle i

Accuracy

Results are accurate to ±3–7 meters depending on the resolution of the source boundary data. Countries with complex coastlines (Norway, Indonesia) may have slightly higher variance.

Data Sources

  • Natural Earth Data (1:10m cultural vectors)
  • OpenStreetMap boundary relations
  • UN GADM administrative boundaries
  • WGS84 ellipsoid parameters (a = 6378137m, f = 1/298.257)
